Shopify 5-Second Webhook Timeout & Auto-Deletion: How to Stop Losing Orders

Everything looks healthy in staging until a flash sale hits or your app rolls out a new container. Suddenly orders/create events start timing out, your ERP stops receiving new orders, and a few hours later Shopify emails your emergency developer contact saying your webhook subscription was removed completely.

Unlike providers that retry quietly for days, Shopify enforces one of the strictest delivery budgets in e-commerce.

1. The 5-Second SLA and 4-Hour Auto-Deletion Window

When Shopify sends a webhook (such as orders/create or inventory_levels/update), your server must fulfill three rules:

  • 5-second response timeout: Your endpoint must return a 2xx HTTP status code within 5 seconds. Taking 5.2 seconds to talk to Postgres, an ERP API, or an email service counts as a failure.
  • No redirects allowed: Returning 301, 302, 307, or 308 (often caused by pointing Shopify to http:// or omitting/adding www) also counts as a failed delivery.
  • 8 retries over 4 hours: Shopify retries failed webhooks up to 8 consecutive times across a 4-hour window with exponential backoff (older legacy guides mention 19 tries / 48 hours, which Shopify shortened). After 8 consecutive failures, Admin API webhook subscriptions are automatically deleted.
Why manual cleanup gets painful fast: Once Shopify deletes an API-registered subscription during a bad 4-hour incident window, restoring your app does not bring the missing orders back automatically. You either run GraphQL/REST Admin reconciliation scripts for every missed merchant or lose order sync silently.

2. The Three Silent Failure Triggers on Shopify Endpoints

Trigger A: Doing ERP, Inventory, or Email Work Inline

If your route verifies the header, runs three SQL queries, calls a shipping label API, and dispatches an email before returning res.status(200), any downstream latency hiccup blows past 5 seconds during checkout bursts.

Trigger B: Parsing JSON Before Checking X-Shopify-Hmac-Sha256

Shopify computes its HMAC SHA-256 signature (encoded in base64) across the exact raw HTTP body bytes. Middleware like standard express.json() or reading await req.json() re-serializes keys and whitespace, turning valid Shopify orders into HTTP 401 rejections that exhaust your 8-retry quota.

// Always verify over raw Buffer bytes in Node / Express
const crypto = require('crypto');

function verifyShopifyHmac(rawBodyBuffer, hmacHeader, shopifySecret) {
  const digest = crypto
    .createHmac('sha256', shopifySecret)
    .update(rawBodyBuffer)
    .digest('base64');

  const a = Buffer.from(digest, 'utf8');
  const b = Buffer.from(hmacHeader || '', 'utf8');
  return a.length === b.length && crypto.timingSafeEqual(a, b);
}

Trigger C: Ignoring Retries on Duplicate Orders (X-Shopify-Webhook-Id)

Because Shopify sends a retry whenever your handler returned a 500, 504, or timed out right after committing a database row, your downstream consumer needs idempotency across X-Shopify-Webhook-Id (or a status-checked dedupe layer so failed attempts are never dropped while delivered orders run once).

3. The Zero-Loss Pattern: Decouple Ingest From Processing

Shopify itself recommends returning 200 OK immediately after queuing the payload. Putting a lightweight ingress buffer in front of your app guarantees Shopify gets its 2xx within milliseconds even if your app server is mid-deploy or your database pool saturates:

  1. Fast Ingress Custody (<10ms): Point Shopify at an ingress proxy backed by durable SQLite WAL storage. It checks X-Shopify-Hmac-Sha256 upfront, commits the raw uncorrupted body to disk, and replies 200 OK immediately so Shopify never drops your subscription.
  2. Concurrency Capping & Auto-Retry: The relay forwards to your real Next.js/Express endpoint under a safe concurrency limit so a flash-sale burst cannot lock up Postgres connections.
  3. Dead-Letter Inspection & 1-Click Replay: Any 500, 502, or 504 stays quarantined with full headers and response errors until your fix is deployed, then replays in one click with deduplication enabled by payload SHA-256.
